About Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam was one of India's most respected scientists, teachers and leaders. He was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. He studied aerospace engineering and later worked with India's leading scientific organizations, including the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). He played an important role in India's space and missile development programmes. He was closely associated with the development of the SLV-III launch vehicle and the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme. His scientific achievements earned him the title "Missile Man of India". In 2002, he became the 11th President of India and served until 2007. He was also a passionate teacher and inspired millions of students to dream big and work for the development of the nation. He received several prestigious awards, including the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian award. His books, speeches and ideas continue to motivate young people. Dr. Kalam passed away on 27 July 2015 while delivering a lecture to students. His life remains an inspiring example of knowledge, hard work, humility and dedication to India.
